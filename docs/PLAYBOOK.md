# Pro playbook

What strong players say they do, and why, in our own words, with links to the sources. Rules marked
**checked** are tested against every game you play (`src/bgcoach/playbook.py`) and appear as
"Playbook" moments when the log shows you did otherwise. The rest need judgment; the coach supports
them with numbers (counter lines, the lobby view, card numbers) instead of a rule.

## Hero pick

From JeefHS, [Ranking ALL Heroes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y4sX0oqgWFE), and Shadybunny,
[The Ultimate Beginner's Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0plivGW2PPo):

- Ask two questions: **does the hero generate economy** (extra gold or cheap minions, e.g. a
  discover for 2 gold), and **how much health does it start with** (health + armor)? Economy gets
  you to tier 5 faster with a decent board, which is where your direction comes from. A hero without
  economy "better be something amazing".
- Avoid hero powers you'll pay gold for without it mattering: that gold is better spent on levelling
  and minions. Some pros take such a hero only for its armor and rarely press the power.
- Health is a resource: more health means more room for mistakes. Low-armor heroes (5–7) are only
  good if their power pays off early (e.g. a hero that wants tier 4 on turn 5); otherwise one or two
  10-damage hits put you on the edge. A no-power hero with lots of health (Patchwerk) beats many weak powers.
- Heroes that do nothing early "roll over and die"; check their early-game strength.
- Many heroes depend on the lobby: some need specific tribes (Beasts, Murlocs and Pirates, an
  "APM" tribe for hand-cycling heroes). The coach shows each offered hero's top-4 rate, early vs late
  fight win rate, starting health, and how this lobby's tribes move its average at your MMR.
- Some heroes change the curve: 1-gold hero powers you want every turn (Captain Eudora, Rafaam…)
  level to tier 2 on turn 3 or 4 instead of turn 2. Learn your hero's curve.

## Economy

From the same two guides:

- **checked** Spend all your gold every turn: a minion, a spell, the hero power, or at worst roll
  the last gold away. Gold doesn't carry over.
- Economy minions (gold, cheap minions, coins) are worth buying early: they let you level and buy
  in the same turn later, and replace your early board faster once you reach tier 4.
- A standard turn-by-turn plan: turn 1 buy; turn 2 level; turn 3 get two units out of 5 gold (an
  economy minion, a 2-gold spell, or selling your turn-1 buy); turn 4 two units; turn 5 level to 3
  and buy; turn 6 level to 4 and spend the last gold on a 1-gold spell, hero power or a roll; turn 7
  is the first real rolling turn (looking for strong 4-drops); tier 5 on turn 8 or 9, staying a turn
  longer on 4 if you're still losing fights.
- Everything you buy before tier 5 exists to keep you alive and set up your real board.

## Early game (turns 1–5)

- **checked** Don't roll in the first five turns (at most once). Build the strongest board your gold
  buys; spend spare gold on tavern spells or levelling. Selling a token to double-buy is fine.
- **checked** Level to tier 2 on turn 2 in 80–90% of games. Otherwise you buy 1-drops while others buy 2-drops.
- **checked** Be on tier 3 by turn 5 at the latest.
- Don't buy trash: a minion is worth buying for standalone tempo, a synergy pair/combo, or economy.
  With none of those in the shop, level instead. A strong pair also raises your triple chances.

## Mid game: stabilize, then commit

- Pick a turn to stabilize: replace early 1- and 2-drops with cards that win fights, so you can level
  safely later. Stabilizing cards often don't make your final board; that's fine.
- **checked** Before levelling, ask "will I fall below 15 HP?" The damage cap means you can't lose more
  than 15 in one fight before top 4, so staying above 15 is effectively two lives.
- Tier 4: look at your board, not the turn number. With a full board, don't stay down to make tiny
  upgrades. With a weak board and low HP, buy stabilizing units first.
- Commit to a direction only when it's enabled (you've hit its scaling engine, not just one tribal
  minion). Your stabilizing buys usually point at a direction.
- Go for big upgrades (a core scaling piece) rather than many small ones.
- Concentrate stats on one or two units rather than spreading them: big units trade into several
  mid-size ones, and the support units are easy to sell later.

## Levelling to 5 and 6

- Know what you're levelling for (a named card, a pair to triple into higher tiers). "Because it's
  shiny" or "for one more damage" isn't a reason.
- Second question: will I die if I level? At high HP, losing one fight is fine; at low HP, stabilize first.
- Late in the game, small upgrades on your current tier stop being worth it: if nothing on this tier
  improves your board much, level.
- **checked** Level before playing a triple reward: it discovers from one tier above your tavern.

## Rolling and freezing

- Before rolling, name the units you'd be happy to find. If that list is short, level instead.
- At 7 gold with nothing buyable on tier 3, rolling for two good units rarely works: level. If one
  unit is buyable, buy it.
- **checked** If you can afford a useful buy, don't roll into something you'll only be able to
  freeze. Rolling your last 1–2 gold is fine: unspent gold is lost.
- **checked** Freeze only what you'll actually buy next turn: a freeze costs you a fresh shop.

## Positioning

From [Positioning Explained by a PRO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AsnJkEs9880) and
[Rank 1 Player Explains EVERY Play](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iUKdMA7jO4s) (JeefHS):

- **checked** Turns 1–7: highest attack first, lowest last. The early game is about value trades
  (a 4/5 kills a 3/4 and survives). Exceptions: minions that summon (put them early to free space for
  the summons) and divine shields.
- Adapt to the taunts players show early: lead with something small into a big divine-shield taunt.
- Late game, **default positioning** when you know nothing about the opponent: a *bumper* (your
  worst-statted unit, e.g. a scaling engine you must keep) first to pop shields and absorb triggers,
  your biggest unit second.
- **checked** One taunt goes in position 7, so a cleave can only hit one minion. With two big taunts,
  use positions 5 and 7; put a minion you don't mind losing next to a taunt ("cleave protect").
- **checked** Against venomous boards (often Murlocs), put your biggest unit last and spend their
  venoms on tokens and mid-size units. Against Beasts and Undead, don't bump: you need every chance
  to reach their backline.
- With information (you've seen their board): value-trade their taunt with your biggest unit and
  save your venom/tech for a higher-impact target.
- Simulate the fight in your head, both with you attacking first and them attacking first, and try
  another order if you don't like the result. (The coach does this for you after the game with the
  simulator: "Order" moments.)
- Late game, give every unit a role: stats, a taunt answer, a venom soak, a protected backline
  scaler. Against boards that trade one-for-one (venom), raw stats are futile: you need *utility*,
  units that trade more than one-for-one (divine shield, cleave).

## Dark Gifts (Season 14)

- From turn 3, pay 3 gold to discover one of three minions, each with a different gift. Once per
  turn, at most 3 times a game. The minion comes from a tier that rises with the turn number
  (turn 3: tier 2; turn 7: tier 4; turn 10: tier 5–6), often above your own tavern tier.
- Early gifts are tempo (big stats on turn 3, scaling-on-play); late ones are powerful (Golemancy,
  Persisting Horror, Titanic Strength 11+). Waiting makes them stronger but costs tempo and HP:
  several top players now press it as soon as it's available.
- Judge the minion as much as the gift: a great gift on a minion that doesn't fit your board is weak.
- Strong-player examples from recent games (JeefHS, dogdog): Harpy's Talons (divine shield +
  windfury) on a strong minion is excellent; Admiration (start of combat: gain the attack of the
  minion to its left) makes positioning matter, so chain Admiration minions behind your biggest attacker.
- **checked** Don't end the game with gifts unused.

## End game

- Prepare your final board early: scaling engines (Brann, Drakkari…) are often weak final units.
  Buy and hold the cards your last fights need, depending on the matchups you expect.
- Go all-in at the right time: on the verge of death, sell scaling for fighting power. Two places
  saved are worth a lot of MMR. When you're winning, knocking out your biggest threat can guarantee first.
- Pivot only into a better-scaling comp, and only from a good spot (high HP and plenty of economy):
  you must be stronger within two turns of pivoting.
